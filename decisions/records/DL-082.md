## Context

`find-missing-amends` (DL-024) lists every decision ID a record's body mentions without declaring it in `amends` or `supersedes`, and `/dld-audit` marks evaluating each candidate as mandatory. Most candidates are plain citations. A full scan of dld-kit's own log gives 182 candidates for 81 records; scans of two larger logs by another agent gave 320 for 209 and 1034 for 435, and a hand check found real missing amendments among a few of them. The noise buries the audit's other findings.

Measured on dld-kit's log, using its 56 declared relationships as known positives:

- A sentence that says "amends DL-N" or "supersedes DL-N" catches 21 of them and flags no undeclared ID.
- A change word such as "replaces" or "no longer" in the same sentence as an ID, in the Decision or Consequences section, catches 6 more and flags 1 undeclared ID. Matching on the same line instead flags 12, all citations, because records are not hard-wrapped and a line is a whole paragraph.
- 9 declared relationships are not mentioned in the body at all, so no scan of the text can find every missing relationship.

## Decision

- `find-missing-amends` classifies each candidate into one tier and prints `<source-id>:<referenced-id>\t<tier>`. A candidate takes its strongest tier.
  - `explicit`: a sentence of the body states the relationship.
    - The body is read without fenced code blocks, inline code spans, and quoted text (between straight or curly double quotes, which cite words rather than state the record's own claim). It is split into sentences after `.`, `;`, `:`, `!` or `?` followed by whitespace, and at line breaks. Text inside parentheses is ignored.
    - The relation words are `amends` and `supersedes`, in any case, not preceded by a letter, digit or hyphen, so `--amends` and `find-missing-amends` do not count.
    - Each relation word claims the IDs after it, up to the next relation word. A sentence with a decision ID before its first relation word states nothing, since another decision is its subject. A later relation word with an ID right before it (`DL-002 supersedes`) states nothing either, and that ID does not belong to the word before.
    - A sentence that ends in a relation word and a colon, with no ID after it ("This decision amends:"), passes the relation to the next sentence in the same section. When that sentence is a list item, the relation covers every item of that list.
    - Known misses, accepted: an ID before the verb ("Unlike DL-012, this amends DL-005"), the passive ("is amended by"), other verbs, and a subject further from the verb ("DL-002's rule supersedes"). A negated sentence ("never supersedes DL-005") is a false positive, so a reader checks each `explicit` candidate before fixing it.
  - `cue`: in the Decision or Consequences section (a `## Decision` or `## Consequences` heading), one of the same sentences that mentions the ID also contains a change cue: a word starting with `replac`, `supersed`, `amend`, `extend`, `overrid`, `relax`, `narrow` or `revert`, or the phrase `no longer` or `instead of`.
  - `mention`: any other mention of the ID anywhere in the body, as DL-024 counts mentions today.
- By default the command prints the `explicit` and `cue` tiers. `--mentions` adds the `mention` tier. `--all` keeps its meaning, every record rather than those changed since the last audit, so the old full output is `--all --mentions`.
- Decision IDs given as arguments check only those records, whatever the audit state. They cannot be combined with `--all`. An ID with no record is an error.
- Pairs that are already related some other way, such as through the reverse direction or a chain of amendments, are not filtered out. On dld-kit's log that would remove 2 of 182 broad candidates.
- `/dld-audit` still runs the check in every audit. An `explicit` candidate is an issue: the record's prose and frontmatter disagree. The audit reads the sentence, reports it, and after the report adds the ID to `amends` or `supersedes` for each fix the user confirms; a new `supersedes` entry also marks that decision `superseded`, and the index is regenerated. `cue` candidates are advisory: the report lists them, and the agent need not evaluate each one. A deep audit, when the user asks for one, runs `--all --mentions`.
- `/dld-audit-auto` fixes `explicit` candidates in the frontmatter, after reading the sentence, and flags each fix in the pull request. A negated sentence, or one about another decision, is listed instead of fixed. It lists `cue` candidates in the pull request without changing anything.
- The sentence rule and the cue words are English. DLD optimises for decision logs written in English. A log in another language gets few or no `explicit` and `cue` candidates, and `--mentions` still lists every mention.

## Rationale

- An explicit sentence costs no judgement to resolve and, on dld-kit's log, has no false positives, so it can be an issue rather than a candidate to weigh.
- The cue tier keeps the likely cases visible without making the agent read every citation. Sentences, not lines, are the unit, because records are written as long unwrapped paragraphs.
- Whether to scan every record and whether to include plain mentions are separate questions, so they are separate flags. A cold start can scan the whole log at the narrow tiers.
- No text scan finds relationships the body never states, so the audit check is a sweep. Catching relationships when a decision is written is the other half.

## Consequences

- Amends DL-024: its output format, `<source-id>:<referenced-id>`, gains the tier column, and by default it no longer prints plain mentions. The changed-since-audit filter and the parsing rules are unchanged.
- A real amendment that the body states in other words ("is now bounded") is no longer flagged by default. It shows up with `--mentions`.
- The command is internal (DL-056), so the output change is not a breaking change for users.
