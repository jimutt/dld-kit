## Context

The spike validates the loop. The full implementation delivers the complete dld-run experience on OpenCode: status line, widget, board overlay, commands, and the findings log. The UI is what differentiates dld-run from a simple auto-continue script.

## Decision

After the spike proves the loop, implement the full UI surface:

- **Status line** — `prompt.footer.status` slot, fed by `statusLine()`
- **Widget** — `sidebar.content` slot, fed by `widgetLines()` (5-line invariant preserved)
- **Board overlay** — `ui.dialog.show` over `boardLines()`, escape-dismissed
- **Commands** — `ctx.command.transform` server-side, mirrored by CLI `keymap.layer` for palette and slash
- **Findings** — `dld_run_note` tool, completion card via toast + sidebar history

The board and findings are deferred from the spike because they depend on the loop working. The widget is in the spike because it's the same mechanism as the status line, just more lines.

## Rationale

The spike answers "can it work?" The full implementation answers "does it feel like dld-run?" The user experience — seeing progress, understanding state, reviewing findings — is the product. The loop is the mechanism.

The board is deferred because it's the most complex UI surface and the least risky technically. Dialogs are documented; the question is whether the loop works at all.

## Consequences

The plugin ships in two phases: spike (loop + minimal UI) and full (complete UI). The spike branch is `feat/opencode-spike`; the full implementation follows on the same branch or a successor.

The transcript card is dropped. OpenCode has no out-of-context entry renderer. Findings surface as toasts and sidebar content instead.
